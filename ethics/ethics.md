# Engineering & Ethics Responsibility Map: Healthcare.gov

## Violated Engineering Principles

* **Reliability and Quality:** As a critical public infrastructure system, the portal needed to be highly stable; the immediate and severe crashes proved that basic quality standards were not met.
* **Verification and Validation:** The platform required thorough functional, integration, and performance testing. Bypassing these steps allowed massive, preventable problems to reach the production environment.
* **Risk Management:** Project and technical risks should have been actively monitored and resolved early on, rather than being allowed to snowball just days before the launch.
* **Professional Competence:** The technical teams had a professional obligation to apply rigorous engineering and testing practices for a system of this complexity, which they failed to do.
* **Public Interest:** Because the platform was built to serve millions of citizens, design and release decisions should have prioritized user impact and system reliability above all else.

## Responsibility Trace

* **CMS / Project Leadership:**
  * *Responsibility:* Overall project coordination, planning, and risk management.
  * *Missed Action:* Failed to maintain strict oversight of the contractors, system readiness, and ongoing risks.
* **Technology Contractors:**
  * *Responsibility:* System development, integration, and testing.
  * *Missed Action:* Failed to adequately test the system and clearly communicate massive technical problems to leadership.
* **Project Managers:**
  * *Responsibility:* Managing the schedule, communication, and resources.
  * *Missed Action:* Failed to recognize and act upon the severe risks caused by delayed software development and rushed testing.
* **Release Decision-Makers:**
  * *Responsibility:* Giving the final approval for public release.
  * *Missed Action:* Proceeded with the October 1 launch without carefully evaluating the massive, unresolved technical risks.

## Ethical & Professional Breaches (C10)

* **Failure to Honestly Communicate Risks:** Multiple contractors, reviewers, and CMS officials knew about the severe capacity issues, software defects, and skipped testing long before launch. However, these risks were never properly escalated or incorporated into the final launch decision. 
* **Sacrificing Reliability for a Deadline:** Decision-makers released a highly complex, interconnected system that they knew was defective simply to meet a fixed political deadline. This violated the core ethical duty of responsible decision-making and failed to protect the public from a highly foreseeable disaster.
