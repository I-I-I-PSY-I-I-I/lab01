# Failure Timeline: Healthcare.gov Launch

## System Overview
* **System Purpose:** Created as part of the Affordable Care Act (ACA), the platform was designed to let citizens verify their eligibility, compare various health insurance plans, apply for financial subsidies, and complete their enrollment.
* **Key Stakeholders:** The Department of Health and Human Services (HHS), the Center for Medicare and Medicaid Services (CMS), government policymakers, tech contractors, insurance providers, and the general public.

## Immediate Failure & Consequences
* **The Failure:** Upon its launch on October 1, 2013, the website immediately suffered from critical functionality and performance breakdowns. 
* **User Impact:** These severe issues prevented the public from reliably creating accounts, viewing information, or finishing their insurance applications.
* **System Consequences:** The intended enrollment process was completely derailed. This forced CMS and its contractors into an emergency recovery mode to drastically overhaul and upgrade system capacity and performance while it was already live.

## Timeline of Major Events
1. **2010 – 2012 (Planning & Construction):** Following the passage of the ACA, CMS took charge of managing multiple contractors to build what would become an increasingly complex technical ecosystem.
2. **2013 (Warning Signs):** Leading up to the launch, several clear red flags emerged. These included the failure of the "Account Lite" feature, reports of poor management, flawed system demonstrations, and reduced project scope. Despite significant concerns regarding system capacity, the project moved forward with only an interim Authority to Operate (ATO).
3. **October – December 2013 (Failure & Emergency Recovery):** The October 1 launch was met with catastrophic system performance. To rescue the failing marketplace, QSSI was brought in to help execute emergency technical work, eventually stabilizing the system by December.
4. **2014 – 2015 (Rebuilding):** Accenture took over operations, bringing in new leadership and making necessary organizational changes. After implementing proper end-to-end testing, the system successfully handled its second enrollment period.
